IRS Says New Founders Are Picking the Wrong Structure Before They Ever File

By Edglex  ·  June 7, 2026

Most first-time founders treat tax planning as something you handle once revenue starts. The IRS is signaling the opposite. Tax Tip 2026-46 frames entity choice and filing setup as a day-zero decision, and the June 5, 2026 IRS release on proposed excise-tax regulations for tax-exempt executive compensation shows how quickly structure and payroll choices can become high-stakes compliance questions. The expensive mistake is not usually fraud. It is sequence.

This is exactly where people miss legal loopholes. They assume loopholes live only in advanced strategies. In practice, many of the highest-value advantages come from choosing the right structure before your first invoice, payroll run, or estimated payment. Once activity starts under the wrong structure, your options narrow and your recordkeeping burden goes up.

What the IRS Actually Said

The IRS is treating setup choices as tax decisions

Tax Tip 2026-46 tells future business owners to choose an entity type intentionally, obtain an EIN when required, and complete employee documentation workflows before hiring. That is not generic business advice; it is filing-risk prevention.

The IRS list is simple: sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, S corporation, and LLC. But the practical difference is not the legal label. It is how each structure changes reporting, payroll obligations, and timing. A founder who assumes they can “fix it later” may discover that fixing it later means amended returns, retroactive payroll corrections, and avoidable professional fees.

The June 5 release on Notice 2026-36 is a useful reminder of why this matters early. Treasury and IRS outlined proposed regulations that expand how certain tax-exempt organization executive compensation rules can apply. Whether or not a new founder is in that exact category today, it demonstrates the same pattern: once compensation design and entity decisions are in motion, retrofitting compliance is harder than getting the structure right up front.

Even the EIN point is frequently misunderstood. People hear EIN and think “bank account paperwork.” The IRS treats it as part of your operating tax identity. If you hire, run payroll, or file business returns without the expected setup, you move from optimization to remediation.

The Hidden Cost: Administrative Drift

Administrative drift is when your business behavior outpaces your tax configuration. You start with side-income assumptions, then add contractors, then add a part-time employee, then open a second revenue channel. None of those steps looks dramatic on its own. Together, they can move you into a filing profile your original structure was not designed for.

Loophole principle: decisions compound

Good loophole strategy is less about one clever move and more about making compounding setup choices that keep your filing path clean when complexity increases.

This is why the IRS keeps emphasizing records from the start. Records are not only proof for audits. They are your ability to claim deductions without guessing and to separate business and personal transactions when your activity grows. If records are weak in year one, the “cost” is usually deferred to year two and year three when your return becomes less forgiving.

How to Apply This Right Now

If you are launching this quarter, run a pre-revenue tax setup checklist before you chase optimization tactics. Confirm entity structure, EIN requirement, payroll status, contractor documentation flow, and your records system. Then check state-level requirements in parallel, because federal and state obligations diverge faster than most new founders expect.

If you already launched and skipped this work, do not wait for year-end. Run a mid-year structure audit now. The best outcome is that nothing changes. The second-best outcome is catching misalignment while adjustments are still straightforward. The worst outcome is finding it during filing season with no time to recover cleanly.

"The earliest tax loophole is not a form trick. It is setting up the right legal and filing path before your growth makes mistakes expensive."
