The IRS Just Published Schedule 1-A. Here's How to Actually Claim the New Deductions.

By Edglex  ·  June 1, 2026

Corrections and updates — 2026-07-12
Corrected the characterization of Schedule 1-A deductions — per IRS FS-2026-04 they reduce taxable income, not adjusted gross income (AGI). Removed "above-the-line" description. Qualified the overtime premium estimation method — the "divide by three" approach is a method described in the Schedule 1-A instructions, not a guaranteed method for all filers.
Source: IRS FS-2026-04, Schedule 1-A, Additional Deductions: What to know about the new form.

The four deductions created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Pub. L. 119-21; specific section numbers: see Schedule 1-A instructions) — no tax on tips, no tax on overtime, no tax on car loan interest, and an enhanced deduction for seniors — are in effect for tax year 2025. They reduce taxable income (not adjusted gross income, per IRS FS-2026-04) and are available whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. And the IRS has now published the form you use to claim them: Schedule 1-A (Form 1040), formally titled "Additional Deductions."

This is the gap that will swallow most eligible taxpayers: knowing a deduction exists is not the same as knowing how to claim it. Schedule 1-A requires specific documentation, specific calculations, and a modified adjusted gross income computation that must be completed before any of the four deduction parts. If you did not know Schedule 1-A existed before sitting down to file, there is essentially no mechanism to find it through standard tax software flows.

What Schedule 1-A Is

Schedule 1-A is a new attachment to Form 1040. Its total carries to Form 1040, reducing your taxable income by the amount you calculate on the schedule. It contains five parts: a MAGI computation in Part I, followed by one part for each of the four deductions (Parts II through V). You complete the parts that apply to your situation. The schedule's instructions include worked examples and worksheets for each deduction.

The structural feature that trips up most filers is Part I — the MAGI calculation. All four deductions phase out at income thresholds, and all four use the same modified adjusted gross income figure as the trigger. You compute your MAGI once, at the start of the form, and that number determines how much of each deduction, if any, phases out before you reach the individual sections. Skipping Part I is not optional — the deductions in Parts II through V reference it.

The Four Deductions and What Each Part Requires

Part II — No Tax on Tips (up to $25,000)

Workers in tip-eligible occupations may deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tip income per return — the same ceiling applies to married couples filing jointly. Qualified tips must have been reported to your employer and received in occupations where tipping is customary; they appear on a W-2, Form 1099-NEC, Form 1099-MISC, or Form 4137. The IRS has published occupational guidance for which industries qualify. The deduction phases out for MAGI above $150,000 (single) or $300,000 (married filing jointly). Documentation required: tip records, employer confirmation, and the applicable income-reporting form showing tip amounts.

Part III — No Tax on Overtime (up to $12,500 / $25,000 MFJ)

The deduction applies to the overtime premium — the "half" in time-and-a-half — not to all compensation paid during overtime hours. Only workers who qualify for overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act are eligible; salaried exempt employees do not qualify. The deduction ceiling is $12,500 for single filers and $25,000 for married filing jointly, with the same $150,000/$300,000 MAGI phase-out thresholds as the tips deduction. If overtime pay is not separated on your W-2, the Schedule 1-A instructions provide a method for estimating the premium component. A working estimate described in those instructions: divide your total overtime compensation by three to approximate the deductible premium — this is an estimate that assumes the regular rate equals the base rate and depends on your specific overtime hours; it should be verified against your actual pay records and the instructions for the year you are filing. Documentation required: W-2 or employer payroll statement distinguishing regular pay from overtime premium amounts.

Part IV — No Tax on Car Loan Interest (up to approximately $10,000)

Interest paid on a qualified passenger vehicle loan may be deducted up to a cap — verify the exact current-year ceiling in the Schedule 1-A instructions. To qualify, the vehicle must be new, assembled in the United States, and the loan must have been originated after December 31, 2024. Used vehicles do not qualify. Vehicles assembled outside the United States do not qualify. Part IV requires the vehicle's VIN and the interest amount from your lender's statement. This deduction has its own income phase-out thresholds, separate from the tips and overtime provisions; check the schedule instructions for current-year MAGI limits. Documentation required: lender's annual interest statement and vehicle identification number.

Part V — Enhanced Senior Deduction ($6,000 per qualifying person)

Taxpayers who were age 65 or older as of December 31, 2025 may claim an additional $6,000 deduction per qualifying individual, for tax years 2025 through 2028. A married couple where both spouses are 65 or older may claim a combined $12,000. The phase-out begins at $75,000 of MAGI for single filers and $150,000 for married filing jointly, reducing by $1 for every $10 of income above the threshold. Part V is the simplest section of the schedule — it asks for filing status and confirmation of age. This deduction stacks on top of the existing additional standard deduction already available to taxpayers over 65 under IRC § 63(f). Documentation required: confirm birthdate on your return; calculate MAGI from Part I to assess phase-out.

"The form to claim these deductions exists. The IRS has published it. The question is whether anyone who qualifies will find it before the filing deadline."

The Documentation Problem

Each deduction requires documentation you may not have already assembled. For tipped income, you need records of the amounts received and how they were reported. For overtime, you need to separate the premium component from total overtime compensation — a calculation your employer's payroll system may not have done for you. For car loan interest, you need the VIN and an interest statement from your lender. For the senior deduction, the math is minimal, but the MAGI phase-out calculation requires assembling your full income picture first.

Gathering this documentation before the filing deadline is significantly easier than gathering it after. Tip logs can be reconstructed from records kept in real time. Overtime estimates require payroll documentation that employers retain for years but do not proactively provide. Car loan interest statements are issued on lender schedules — if you need a corrected statement, requesting it takes time. The earlier you start, the less you are doing under pressure.

Why This Still Won't Reach Most People

The IRS has published Schedule 1-A and its instructions. The information is in the public record. But the publication of a tax form is not the same as the provision reaching the people who qualify for it. Standard tax software flows are designed around common deductions: mortgage interest, charitable contributions, retirement account contributions. A new form, attached to new provisions targeting specific categories of workers, will not surface automatically in the default walkthrough of most software packages.

The asymmetry is structural. Tax professionals working with clients in tipped industries, overtime-eligible roles, or recent auto purchases will know to look for Schedule 1-A. The majority of eligible workers who file independently will not, unless they go looking.
